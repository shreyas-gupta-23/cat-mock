Percentages

Successive change, constancy, elections — no fraction-to-% tables.

01

Sheet

Skip converting \(3/8=37.5\%\). CAT is change-of-base and chained multipliers.

\[(1+\tfrac{a}{100})(1+\tfrac{b}{100})(1+\tfrac{c}{100})-1\]

Two-step net (signed \(a,b\)):

\[a+b+\tfrac{ab}{100}\]

Equal successive \(x\%\) giving net \(N\%\): \(2x+x^2/100=N\). Three equal: \((1+x/100)^3-1=N/100\).

More / less inversion. If \(A\) is \(p\%\) more than \(B\), then \(B\) is \(\frac{100p}{100+p}\%\) less than \(A\). If \(A\) is \(p\%\) less than \(B\), \(B\) is \(\frac{100p}{100-p}\%\) more than \(A\).

\(x\%\) of \(A = y\%\) of \(B\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(A:B = y:x\). “\(A\) is \(p\%\) of \(B\)” is not “\(A\) is \(p\%\) more than \(B\)”.

Product constancy (price \(\times\) consumption = spend). Price \(+p\%\), spend fixed \(\Rightarrow\) consumption \(-\frac{100p}{100+p}\%\). If one factor \(+x\%\) and the other \(-x\%\), product falls \((x/10)^2\%\).

Numerator \(+p\%\), denominator \(+q\%\): fraction multiplies by \(\frac{100+p}{100+q}\).

Elections. Valid \(=\) polled \(-\) invalid. Winner’s votes \(=\frac12(\text{valid}+\text{margin})\). Margin as % of valid is not margin as % of polled.

Two tests. Fail \(E\), fail \(M\), fail both: inclusion-exclusion. Pass both \(= 100 - (e+m-b)\).

From CAT 2017–2020. Every % question on this page is one of these moves.

  • Remainder chain. After a filter of \(p\%\), leftover is \((1-p/100)\) of the current base. Two filters in sequence: leftover \(= N(1-a)(1-b)\). “20% of the rest” is never 20% of the original. Invert from the last leftover: shirts 8840 after 15% defect then 20% of rest domestic \(\Rightarrow N=8840/(0.85\times 0.80)\).
  • Product split. Production \(=\) population \(\times\) per capita. Multipliers divide: population factor \(=\) production factor / per-capita factor. \(+40\%\) production and \(+27\%\) per capita \(\Rightarrow\) population \(\times 1.40/1.27\).
  • Falls short of the pass mark by \(k\%\) means scored \((1-k/100)\) of the pass mark, not of \(N\). Pass \(=0.45N\), scored 36 \(=0.32\times 0.45N\) if 68% short of pass.
  • Two groups + a count. \(g=0.6n\), \(b=0.4n\), \(g-b=30\) pins \(n\). Then a second % on the same \(n\) (who passed) is a new equation, not a new base unless they say so.
  • Review of a score is successive % on marks. “Fails by 35” and “7 more than pass after another hike” is two linear equations in (original marks, pass marks).
  • \(A\) is \(p\%\) more than \(B\) and \(q\%\) less than \(C\). \(A=B(1+p/100)=C(1-q/100)\). Then apply fresh % changes to \(B\) and \(C\) and compare the new pair.
  • Two-category growth. \(F+N=T_0\), \(1.1F+1.12N=T_1\). Two equations, two stocks. They will ask the later stock, not the earlier, half the time.
  • Score chain. Write every person as a multiplier of the last free variable: \(A=0.9B\), \(B=1.25C\), \(C=0.8D\). One given score pins all.
Trap. Successive % sit on different bases. 10% of a 20% increment is 2% of the original, not 30% stacked. Weighted % of two groups is never \((p_1+p_2)/2\) unless \(n_1=n_2\). “Short by 68%” is not “scored 32% of maximum.”
Move. Convert every % into a multiplier on 100 or on a letter, then multiply. Restore the original only at the end. Work backwards from the last leftover.
