\[\sum\alpha=-\tfrac{b}{a},\;\prod\alpha=\tfrac{c}{a}\quad\text{for }ax^2+bx+c=0\]

\(D=b^2-4ac\). Vertex at \(x=-b/(2a)\). For \(x+1/x=k\), \(x^2+1/x^2=k^2-2\), \(x^3+1/x^3=k^3-3k\). If \(a+b+c=0\) then \(a^3+b^3+c^3=3abc\).

\[a^3\pm b^3=(a\pm b)(a^2\mp ab+b^2),\quad (a+b+c)^2=\sum a^2+2\sum ab\]

AM–GM: \(\frac{x_1+\cdots+x_n}{n}\ge\sqrt[n]{x_1\cdots x_n}\). Weighted AM–GM. For \(x>0\), \(x+\frac{k}{x}\ge 2\sqrt{k}\).

\(|x-a|<b\iff a-b<x<a+b\). \(|x-a|>b\iff x<a-b\) or \(x>a+b\). Squaring inequalities: both sides non-negative, or split.

\(f\circ g\): inner first. Inverse iff injective on the stated domain. \(f(x)=|x-a|+|x-b|\) minimises on \([a,b]\) (the interval between).

Logs (CAT): \(\log_b a = \frac{\log_k a}{\log_k b}\), \(\log_b a\cdot\log_a b=1\), \(\log_b a^c=c\log_b a\), change of base. Domain \(a>0,a\ne 1\) for the base; argument \(>0\).

AP: \(T_n=a+(n-1)d\), \(S_n=\frac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]\). GP: \(T_n=ar^{n-1}\), \(S_n=a\frac{r^n-1}{r-1}\). Special: \(1+r+r^2+\cdots=\frac{1}{1-r}\) for \(|r|<1\) (rare in CAT; finite GP is the default).

Integer roots: rational root theorem. Max/min of quadratic on integers: evaluate near the vertex.

  • Logs. \(\log_b a=1/\log_a b=\log a/\log b\). \(\log_b a\cdot\log_c b\cdot\log_a c=1\). If \(\log_3 x=\log_{12} y=a\) then \(x=3^a\), \(y=12^a\), GM \(=\sqrt{xy}=6^a\), so \(\log_6 G=a\). Nested: \(\log_2(5+\log_3 a)=3\) unwraps inside-out. \(1/\log_k 100=\log_{100} k\), so a long alternating chain is \(\log_{100}\) of a product/quotient of the bases.
  • Change to one base when \(p^3=q^4=r^5=s^6=k\). \(p=k^{1/3}\) etc. Then \(\log_s(pqr)=\frac13/\frac16+\frac14/\frac16+\frac15/\frac16\).
  • Exponents to a common base. \(9^{2x-1}-81^{x-1}=1944\) is \(3^{4x-2}-3^{4x-4}\). \(2^{6x}+2^{3x+2}-21=0\) is quadratic in \(2^{3x}\). \(a^{m}=b^{n}=c^{p}\) take logs, or write all as \(k\).
  • \(x+1/x=k\) substitution on \((x+1/x)^2-3(x+1/x)+2=0\). Then solve \(x+1/x=1\) or 2 and discard \(x=0\).
  • \(f(ab)=f(a)f(b)\) on positives. Completely multiplicative. \(f(1)=f(1)^2\Rightarrow f(1)\in\{0,1\}\); largest is 1. If \(f(1),f(2),f(3)\) positive integers, \(f(24)=54=2\cdot 3^3\) pins the primes: \(f(2)^3 f(3)=54\), with \(f(1)<f(2)\).
  • Even about a point. \(f(5+x)=f(5-x)\) \(\Rightarrow\) roots symmetric about 5. Four distinct roots: they pair as \(5\pm a\), \(5\pm b\), sum \(20\).
  • min / max of two graphs. \(f=\min\{2x^2, 52-5x\}\): max of \(f\) is at the intersection of the two, in the positive reals. \(f=\max\{5x, 52-2x^2\}\): min of \(f\) is at their intersection.
  • \(|f+g|=|f|+|g|\) iff \(fg\ge 0\). For \(f=2x-5\), \(g=7-2x\), that is \(x\) between the two roots.
  • Quadratic always positive: \(a>0\) and \(D<0\) (or \(D=0\) if \(\ge\)). Two such, maximise a linear in the coefficients.
  • \(|x^2-x-6|=x+2\) split on the kinks of the abs. Distinct roots, then product.
  • \((p(x))^{q(x)}=1\) cases: base 1; base \(-1\) and even exponent; exponent 0 with base \(\ne 0\). For a product of two integer-valued quadratics equal to 1, the integer pairs are \((1,1)\) and \((-1,-1)\) (each quadratic ranging in a small set).
  • AP. \(T_7^2=T_3 T_{17}\) with \(d>0\) pins \(a:d\). Partial sum of first \(n\) vs first \(3n\). Telescoping \(\frac1{(3k-1)(3k+2)}=\frac13\bigl(\frac1{3k-1}-\frac1{3k+2}\bigr)\). Common terms of two APs: AP with difference lcm, first common term, last \(\le\) both ends.
  • GP infinite: \(a_n=3(a_{n+1}+a_{n+2}+\cdots)=3\cdot a_{n+1}/(1-r)\). Plus sum 32 pins \(a,r\).
  • GP in \(x,y,z\), and 5x, 16y, 12z in AP: \(y^2=xz\), \(32y=5x+12z\). Let \(y=xr\), \(z=xr^2\).
  • Recurrence \(x_{m}=x_{m+1}+(m+1)\) with \(x_1=-1\) is a backward sum: \(x_{100}=-1-\sum_{k=2}^{100}k\).
  • Fibonacci-like \(f(x+2)=f(x)+f(x+1)\). Work backwards from two given terms; the linear recurrence runs both ways.
  • Linear Diophantine. \(2x+5y=99\), \(x\ge y\ge -20\). Particular solution, then the general step (\(+5,-2\)), count lattice points in the band.
  • Unique solution of two linears iff determinant \(k^2-4\ne 0\), i.e. \(|k|\ne 2\).
  • Toffees: each child gets half the remaining plus one, five children empty the stock. Work backwards: before the last child there was 1 (because \(\frac12 s+1=s\Rightarrow s=2\)? Last child takes all: remaining after 4 is 2 if the rule is “half plus one empties”). Standard: if after taking half plus one nothing is left, remaining going in was 2. Unfold five times.
Trap. \(x^2>9\Rightarrow x>3\) is false. \(\log(ab)=\log a+\log b\) needs \(a,b>0\). Cancelling \((x-1)\) loses the root \(x=1\). \(a^0=1\) fails at \(a=0\).
Move. Factor or substitute (\(t=x+1/x\), \(t=\sqrt{x}\), \(t=2^{3x}\)). If it looks messy, an identity is sitting there.
