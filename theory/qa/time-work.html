CAT 2026 Arithmetic 15 PYQs

Time and work

Rates, pipes, alternate days, wages.

01

Sheet

Rate of A in \(a\) days: \(1/a\) job per day. LCM-of-days as total units is the same model with integers.

\[\frac{1}{T}=\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{c}\quad(\text{c a leak})\]

Efficiency ratio \(e_A:e_B\) \(\Rightarrow\) time ratio \(e_B:e_A\). “\(A\) is \(p\%\) more efficient than \(B\)” \(\Rightarrow\) rates \(100+p:100\), not times \(100+p:100\).

Alternate days: work in a 2-day cycle, leftover < one cycle handled separately. If they start with A, the last partial day may be A’s or B’s — check leftover against one person’s rate.

Pipes: inlet \(+\), outlet \(-\). A cistern fills in \(a\), empties in \(b\): net \(1/a-1/b\). Overflow after a tap is closed: split the timeline.

Men-women-children: convert to one unit (man-days). \(m_1\times d_1\times h_1 / w_1 = m_2\times d_2\times h_2 / w_2\) if efficiency is uniform.

Wages \(\propto\) work done (rate \(\times\) time actually worked), not \(\propto\) days present if rates differ.

From CAT 2017–2020.

  • One new equal worker every day. Day \(k\) has \(k\) people of rate \(1/120\). Work after \(n\) days: \(\frac{1}{120}\cdot\frac{n(n+1)}{2}=1\) \(\Rightarrow n(n+1)=240\), \(n=15\).
  • Inlet 8 h, inlet+outlet 10 h. Outlet rate \(=1/8-1/10=1/40\) tank/h. Full to half-full with only outlet: 20 h.
  • Humans and robots, two mixes. \(15h+5r=1/30\), \(5h+15r=1/60\). Linear. Then 15 humans alone.
  • Fill pipes vs drain pipes, two scenarios. \(6f-5d=1/6\), \(5f-6d=1/60\). Then \(2f-d\).
  • Two types of inlet. Same idea, both positive: \(10A+45B=1/30\), \(8A+18B=1/60\) (per minute).
  • Clock: tank emptied at a fixed time, then A and/or B fill. Monday A done at 8 pm, Tuesday B at 6 pm, Wednesday A till 5 then B to 7. The empty-time is the unknown start \(T\). Three equations in \(T, a, b\) as hours of work. Thursday both: time \(T + 1/(1/a+1/b)\).
  • Efficiency drop after day 7. Together 16 days usually. After 7, Ramesh at 70%. They finish on day 17. Remaining after 7 is 9/16; the extra day is because of the 30% cut. Ganesh-alone on that remaining is leftover / Ganesh’s rate.
  • Half job A, then 4 days together, then B finishes the last 5%. B is 25% slower than A. Let A’s days be \(a\), B’s \(1.25a\). The 5% leftover pins \(a\).
  • A half as efficient, B triple, 9 days vs usual 12 together. \(A+B=1/12\), \(A/2+3B=1/9\).
  • Men vs machines. \(3m+8M\) takes half the time of \(8m+3M\) \(\Rightarrow\) rate ratio 2:1. Two machines in 13 days \(\Rightarrow\) one machine’s rate, then how many men match that in 13 days.
  • Third person does 10% of the job. Anil 3 days, then Sunil joins, then Bimal. Work remaining after Anil’s 3 is 17/20. Bimal’s 10% is 0.1 of the whole job, not of the leftover.
  • MDH leftover. 6 km in 200 days, 140 people, 1.5 km in 60 days. Remaining 4.5 km in 140 days. People needed \(=\) (140 \(\times\) 60 / 1.5) \(\times\) 4.5 / 140, then extra \(=\) that minus 140.
  • Overtime at 2\(\times\) wage, 15% of regular income. \(114\,o = 0.15\times 57\,r\) and \(r+o=172\).
Trap. “Together they would have finished in 10, but B leaves after 4” — do not assign 4 days of combined rate to both remaining. Compute work done, then leftover / leftover person’s rate.
Move. Units of work first. Then who is on stage in each interval.
