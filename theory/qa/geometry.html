Geometry

Similar triangles, circles, 3-D scale, coordinate.

01

Sheet

Similar triangles: AA. Sides proportional; areas square of the scale. Midline of a triangle is parallel to the third side and half as long. Median splits area equally; centroid divides each median \(2:1\).

Right triangle: Pythagoras. \(30^circ\)-\(60^circ\)-\(90^circ\) \(=1:\sqrt{3}:2\). \(45^circ\)-\(45^circ\)-\(90^circ\) \(=1:1:\sqrt{2}\). Area \(\frac12 ab\sin C = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\).

Circle: angle in a semicircle \(90^circ\). Centre angle \(=2\times\) circumference angle on the same arc. Tangent \(\perp\) radius; two tangents from an external point equal. Chord \(=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}\). Power of a point: \(PA\cdot PB=PC\cdot PD=\mathrm{tangent}^2\).

Cyclic quadrilateral: opposite angles sum \(180^circ\). Ptolemy if needed: \(AC\cdot BD=AB\cdot CD+AD\cdot BC\).

Coordinate: distance, midpoint, slope; perpendicular \(m_1m_2=-1\). Section formula: point dividing \(A,B\) in \(m:n\) is \(\bigl(\frac{nx_1+mx_2}{m+n},\frac{ny_1+my_2}{m+n}\bigr)\). Circle \((x-h)^2+(y-k)^2=r^2\).

Mensuration: cylinder \(\pi r^2 h\), cone \(\frac13\pi r^2 h\), sphere \(\frac43\pi r^3\), CSA sphere \(4\pi r^2\). Scale \(k\): area \(k^2\), volume \(k^3\).

Regular polygon interior \(\frac{(n-2)180}{n}\). Number of diagonals \(\frac{n(n-3)}{2}\).

  • Diamond \(|x|+|y|=2\). Area 8 (square with diagonal 4). Inequality \(|x|+|y|\le 2\) minus the strip \(|x|<1\) is a hexagon; compute as diamond minus two triangles, or pick’s / geometry of the band \(|x|\ge 1\).
  • Centroid. Medians divide a triangle into six small triangles of equal area. Cutting GBC off ABC leaves 2/3 of the area (the three small ones toward A). Area of 40-25-35 by Heron, then \(\times 2/3\).
  • Distance from a vertex to the hypotenuse is the altitude: \(\frac{ab}{c}\). Min time at 30 km/h is that length over 30 (in hours, then \(\times 60\)).
  • Right isosceles, hypotenuse as diameter of a semicircle, plus an arc centred at A. Region between the semicircle and the arc: \(\frac12\pi (c/2)^2 - \bigl(\frac14\pi c^2 - \frac12 a^2\bigr)\) — draw it; CAT 2017 S1 Q17 lands on 18, the two pizza-slices cancelling to the right triangle.
  • Cube melted into five cubes 1:1:8:27:27 in volume. Side scale is cube root: 1,1,2,3,3. Surface \(\propto\) side². Original volume 1+1+8+27+27=64, side 4. % extra surface \(= (1+1+4+9+9)/16 - 1\).
  • Frustum / cut cone. Similar: height 12, cut 3 from the tip (9 from the base). Remaining volume \(=\) original minus small similar cone of height 3. Scale 3/12=1/4, volume factor 1/64.
  • Cone cut parallel at 18 from the base, height 27. Small cone height 9, scale 1/3, volume 1/27 of original. Two pieces: small cone vs frustum. Difference 225 pins the original.
  • Regular hexagon side 1, square on AC. AC is \(\sqrt{3}\) (two equilateral heights? In hexagon AC skips one vertex: length \(\sqrt{3}\)). Square area 3.
  • Rectangle in a circle, diagonal = diameter = 26. \(l^2+b^2=26^2\). (24,10) works, (25,9) does not (625+81=706\(\ne\)676).
  • Two parallel chords same side, 4 and 6, gap 1. \(r^2=(d)^2+3^2=(d+1)^2+2^2\).
  • Chord length \(2r\sin(\theta/2)\). 5 cm at 60° \(\Rightarrow\) the 120° chord is \(5\sqrt{3}\).
  • Power / intersecting chords. Diameter 22, chord 20.5, CE=7 so ED=15. \(AE\cdot EB=CE\cdot ED=105\). Let AE\(=x\), EB\(=20.5-x\), \(|EB-AE|\).
  • Angle in a semicircle + inscribed. Cyclic ABCD, \(\angle COD=120\) is centre on arc CD so inscribed is 60; chase \(\angle BCD\).
  • Incircle of a rhombus. A rhombus has an incircle. \(r=\mathrm{area}/s\) with \(s\) the semiperimeter. Diagonals 12, 16: area 96, side 10, \(s=20\), \(r=4.8\). Circle-to-rhombus area ratio \(\pi(4.8)^2/96=6\pi/25\).
  • Medians perpendicular, 12 and 9. Area of triangle \(=\frac43\times\frac12\times 12\times 9=72\) (four small right triangles of legs 8 and 6 around the centroid, since centroid splits 2:1).
  • Equilateral, sum of perpendiculars from an interior point = height. Viviani. Area \(= s^2 / \sqrt{3}\) if \(s\) is that height? Height \(s\), area \(s^2/\sqrt{3}\). Yes CAT option.
  • Chord through (3 E, 4 N) of O, radius 5. Power: distance OM from O to the chord (line AB). A is 4 north, B 3 east, chord is the line through them. Distance from O to AB, then chord \(=2\sqrt{r^2-d^2}\).
  • Concentric: chord of C1 tangent to C2, length 6, diameters differ by 2. \(r_1^2 - r_2^2 = 3^2\), \(r_1=r_2+1\), so \(2r_2+1=9\), \(r_2=4\), diameter of C1 is 10.
  • Circumcircle of (0,0),(4,0),(3,9). Perp bisectors, or formula \(R=abc/(4K)\).
  • Parallelogram opposite vertices (2,1), (−3,−4); other two on a line. Midpoint of both diagonals coincides: midpoint \((\frac{-1}{2},\frac{-3}{2})\) lies on the line of the other two, and the line is given as \(x+9y+c=0\) so plug the midpoint after finding the two points — or: the other two have midpoint that same, so the given line must pass through that midpoint, pinning \(c\).
  • Grid paths (1,1)\(\to\)(8,10) via (4,6), only right and up: binomial \(\binom{3+5}{3}\times\binom{4+4}{4}\).
  • Trapezium AB ∥ DC, BC ⊥ DC, \(\angle BAD=45^\circ\), DC=5, BC=4. Height 4, the 45° makes the overhang 4, so AB=5+4=9 (or |5-4| depending which way). Area \(=\frac{AB+DC}{2}\times h\).
  • Infinite midpoint triangles of an equilateral. Each area is 1/4 of the previous. Sum \(A/(1-1/4)=\frac43 A\) with \(A=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}\times 24^2=144\sqrt{3}\), total \(192\sqrt{3}\).
  • Brick face diagonals 3 : \(2\sqrt{3}\) : \(\sqrt{15}\). \(l^2+b^2\), \(b^2+h^2\), \(h^2+l^2\) in that ratio. Add pairwise to isolate edges, then shortest:longest.
  • Square pyramid, equilateral triangular faces, side 20. Apex is directly above the square centre. Height from \(10\sqrt{2}\) (half-diagonal) and slant 20: \(h=\sqrt{20^2-10^2-10^2}=10\sqrt{2}\).
  • Max altitude to hypotenuse of a right triangle with hypotenuse 20 is 10 (isosceles).
Trap. “Each edge \(+10\%\)” volumes \(+33.1\%\), not \(+10\%\) and not \(+30\%\). Height of equilateral is \(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a\), not \(a\). Rectangle in a circle needs \(l^2+b^2=(\text{diameter})^2\), not just “looks Pythagorean.”
Move. Draw, mark equal angles, hunt one similar pair. If numbers are ugly, drop an altitude or use coordinates.
