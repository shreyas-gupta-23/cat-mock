Number system

Remainders, factors, last digits, bases.

01

Sheet

\(n=dq+r\), \(0\le r<d\). \(a\equiv b\pmod m\Rightarrow m\mid(a-b)\). Linear \(ax\equiv b\pmod m\) solvable iff \(\gcd(a,m)\mid b\), then that many solutions.

CRT: pairwise coprime moduli, unique class modulo the product.

\[\mathrm{lcm}(a,b)\cdot\gcd(a,b)=ab\quad(a,b>0)\]

\(n=\prod p_i^{a_i}\): divisors \((a_1+1)(a_2+1)\cdots\). Sum of divisors \(\prod\frac{p^{a+1}-1}{p-1}\). Odd divisors: ignore \(2^{a}\). Square \(\iff\) all \(a_i\) even. Cube \(\iff\) all \(a_i\) multiples of 3.

Last digit: cycles. \(2,3,7,8\): 4. \(4,9\): 2. \(0,1,5,6\): 1. Last two digits: mod 100. Euler \(\varphi(100)=40\) for coprime bases.

Trailing zeros of \(n!\): \(\sum_{k\ge 1}\lfloor n/5^k\rfloor\). Highest power of \(p\) in \(n!\): \(\sum \lfloor n/p^k\rfloor\).

Remainders of \(a^n\): binomial \((1+m)^n\) or Euler/Fermat. \(7^{n}\bmod 4 = (-1)^n\bmod 4\).

HCF of \(a,b\) = HCF\((a,b-ka)\). HCF of \(n,n+k\) divides \(k\). For \(f(n)=an+b\), HCF of consecutive values often divides a constant.

Base \(b\): \((d_k\cdots d_0)_b = \sum d_i b^i\), each \(d_i<b\).

From CAT 2017–2020.

  • Egyptian fraction. \(\frac1a+\frac1b=\frac19\), \(a\le b\): \(b=\frac{9a}{a-9}\), so \(a-9\) divides 81. Count positive divisors.
  • Difference of squares. \(n^2-m^2=105=(n-m)(n+m)\). Factor pairs of 105 of the same parity, both positive, \(n+m>n-m\).
  • \(ab=4^{2017}\) with \(a\le b\). Number of factor pairs is \(\lceil(e+1)/2\rceil\) where \(e=4034\) is the exponent of 2, i.e. 2018.
  • Perfect-square factors of \(2^4 3^5 10^4=2^8 3^5 5^4\) greater than 1: even exponents up to the max, minus the \(2^0 3^0 5^0\) case.
  • Three consecutive product 15600. Near cube root 25; check 24·25·26.
  • \((a-b)^2\) identity / sum of squares. \(a^2+b^2=97\), product \(p\): \((a+b)^2=97+2p\ge 0\), \((a-b)^2=97-2p\ge 0\) so \(|p|\le 48.5\). 64 is impossible.
  • \((a^3-b^3)/(a-b)^3=157/3\), \(ab=616\). \(\frac{a^2+ab+b^2}{(a-b)^2}=\frac{157}{3}\), and \(a^2+2ab+b^2=(a+b)^2\), \(a^2+b^2=(a+b)^2-2ab\).
  • Dot product max. \(a^2+b^2=25\), \(x^2+y^2=169\), \(ax+by=65=5\cdot 13\) \(\Rightarrow\) the two vectors are parallel, so \(ay-bx=0\).
  • Ascending-digit numbers from 1–9, no repeat: each non-empty subset of size \(\ge 2\) is one number. \(2^9-1-9=502\).
  • Digit product in \((2,7)\): 3-digit, product 3,4,5,6. Casework on factorisations into three digits 1–9.
  • 7 before 3 in a 4-digit with distinct digits. Among positions of 7 and 3, half the equally likely orders have 7 first (careful with 0). Count total distinct 4-digit with both 7 and 3, divide by 2, plus numbers with 7 and not 3, etc. — or: pick 4 distinct, if both 7 and 3 present then 2 of 2! orders on those two seats work.
  • Form \(aabb\) even. \(1100a+11b=11(100a+b)\), \(b\) even, \(a=1..9\). Mean of that list.
  • Two-digit \(10x+y>3(10y+x)\), \(y\ne 0\). \(7x>29y\), small loop on \(y\).
  • Pigeonhole birthdays. 100 people, 12 months, minimise the maximum month: \(\lceil 100/12\rceil=9\).
  • Inclusion-exclusion. How many of 1..120 divisible by none of 2, 5, 7.
  • AM–GM in disguise. \(x+y=102\), minimise \((1+1/x)(1+1/y)=1+103/(xy)\). Minimum when \(xy\) is maximum, i.e. \(x=y=51\).
  • Magic square 1–9. Line sum 15. Corners 6 and 2 given; centre is 5; bottom middle follows.
  • Wrong factor 73 vs 37, product up 720. Other two numbers multiply to \(720/(73-37)=20\). Min \(p^2+q^2\) given \(pq=20\): 2 and 10 \(\to 4+100=104\)? Reals: min when \(p=q=\sqrt{20}\), sum of squares 40. Stem says three real numbers, so 40.
Trap. Remainder when \(2n\) is divided by 5 is not twice the remainder of \(n\) unless you reduce mod 5. Number of even factors is not “half the factors” if \(n\) is odd (then zero) or if \(n=2^k\times\text{odd}\).
Move. Factor first. For remainders, rewrite the dividend as a nearby multiple plus a small integer, or factor \(a^n-1\).
