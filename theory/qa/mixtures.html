Mixtures

Alligation, replacement, three-vessel loops.

01

Sheet

Alligation (cheap \(c\), dear \(d\), mean \(m\), \(c<m<d\)):

\[\frac{q_c}{q_d}=\frac{d-m}{m-c}\]

Same cross for concentrations, % milk, even for speeds if the stem is a weighted mean in disguise.

Replacement. Vessel \(V\), draw \(x\), refill, \(n\) times. Fraction of original solute left \((1-x/V)^n\). Quantity left \(V(1-x/V)^n\) if it started pure. From the second draw you remove mixture, not pure solute — the formula already encodes that.

If you replace with another solution of strength \(p\) instead of water, leftover original still \((1-x/V)^n\); the added component is a geometric series.

Three vessels: write three linear equations in milk/water. CAT often gives “poured into a fourth empty” — totals add.

From CAT 2017–2020 (this page also caught some ratio / average stems via 2IIM’s combined tag).

  • Alligation on milk fractions. Bottle 1 is \(7/9\) milk, bottle 2 is \(9/13\), target \(3/4\). Mix ratio \(=\) distances to the target.
  • Three mixes, then mix those. Convert each to a water-fraction, weight by 4 : 3 : 2, compare resulting water vs B vs C. Do not alligate three at once.
  • Replacement twice. Alcohol left \(=700\times(0.9)^2\) from 875 ml. Water is the complement. Strength \(p\%\) means \(p\) grams in 100 ml — treat as a concentration, same algebra.
  • A \(\to\) B \(\to\) C \(\to\) A transfers. After each 100 ml move, the source concentration updates before the next draw. Track grams of salt in each vessel, not just %.
  • Add water to change dye:water from 2:3 to 2:5 (dye fixed). Then remove 1/4 of the new mix (dye and water both drop 25%), then add dye to restore 2:3.
  • Mix 1:3, then double the volume by adding more of A. Write two stages. Unknown % of B is one linear equation.
  • Volume vs weight. Alloy 3:4:7 by volume, densities 5:2:6. Weight of C in 130 kg is the C-share of \(\sum v_i d_i\).
  • Density of a blend. 1 L of liquid 1 is 1 kg, liquid 2 is 0.8 kg; 0.5 L of mix is 0.48 kg \(\Rightarrow\) volume fraction from a weighted density.
  • Elevator bound. All between 53 and 57 kg, total \(\le 630\). Max people: assume as light as possible except the stated heaviest must fit — \(53(n-1)+57\le 630\).
  • Lowest 9 mean 42, highest 9 mean 47, ten students. Sum of 10 \(=\) (sum of lowest 9) + max \(=\) (sum of highest 9) + min. Max \(-\) min is fixed by those two means; the gap between possible means of the ten is max\(-\)min minus the forced overlap.
  • Cricket: \(n+2\) innings, last two 38 and 15, first \(n\) average 30, each \(<38\), minimise the lowest. Total of first \(n\) is \(30n\); to min \(x\), dump as much as possible into the other \(n-1\) without hitting 38.
Trap. “8 L replaced twice from 40 L” is not “16 L milk gone.” Milk gone is \(40-40(0.8)^2=14.4\).
Move. Track one component (usually milk or acid). Water is the complement if the vessel stays full.
